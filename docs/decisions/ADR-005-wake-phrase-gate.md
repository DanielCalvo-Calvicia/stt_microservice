# ADR-005: The wake-phrase gate is a second service instance with a local engine, under `/gate`

- **Status:** Accepted and built (commit `e366de1`); not run with a real model in the last pass.
- **Date:** 2026-10-05.

## Context

With the OpenAI engine every utterance the microphone hears costs tokens, including room noise and conversations that are not addressed to the robot. Brain wants to answer only when the wake phrase (for example "Oblivion 306") is heard, and to pay for the real engine only then. The decision about what the text means belongs to Brain.

## Decision

With `STT_GATE_ENABLED=1` the service builds a second `SttService` with its own queue and a small local engine (`STT_GATE_MODEL`, default `base` since 2026-11-09, greedy decoding, `STT_GATE_PROMPT` as a hint). Its routes are the same as the main ones with the prefix `/gate` (`set`, `get`, `available`, `stop`; no batch and no single-request stream). Its `completed` events carry the utterance audio in `audio_base64` (contracts 0.11.0). Brain reads the text, decides, and sends the audio of an utterance that held the phrase to the real engine through `POST /process/batch`. `/available` is true only when both engines are ready. The gate is off by default.

## Consequences

- STT does not know the wake phrase or decide anything: the gate only returns text plus audio, so STT calls no other service ([ADR-006](ADR-006-no-direct-service-calls.md)).
- The gate's model is a second model in memory and needs a download on first use (`scripts/fetch_gate_model.py`, which in Docker fetches nothing at build time; see [deployment](../deployment/deployment.md)).
- Both streams are independent: stopping one does not stop the other (`/stop` and `/gate/stop`); shutdown stops both.
- The gate needs the same on Brain (`WAKE_PHRASE_ENABLED=1`) and the same words in `STT_GATE_PROMPT` and Brain's `WAKE_PHRASE`.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (T-01.1.7):** run for real with a real microphone through Brain (microphone, stt, tts, speaker, Brain; no ai-agent, no stepper). The decision holds: a sentence without the phrase produced no `/process/batch` request, one with it produced exactly one, carrying the gate's own audio, and stt logged lengths only. The weak point is the gate's recognition of the name, not the design: `tiny.en` heard `Lulion` for `Oblivion` (similarity 0.57 against the 0.75 threshold) and `base.en` heard the phrase 1 time in 3 (twice the name was missing: `306, what time is it?`). Knobs, all settings: `STT_GATE_MODEL`, `STT_GATE_PROMPT`, the wake phrase itself. Brain's `gate_heard` log field writes the text of ignored speech to Brain's log.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (gate tuning, the defaults changed):** `STT_GATE_MODEL` now defaults to `base` (was `tiny.en`) and `STT_GATE_PROMPT` to `Oblivion 306.` (was `Oblivion`). Evidence, offline, on 16 clips of the maintainer's voice from the real microphone service (10 saying the phrase, 6 ordinary sentences), scored with Brain's own matcher: the prompt `Oblivion 306.` (name and code) lifted `tiny.en` from 0 to 5 and `base.en` from 0 to 8 phrases of 10, `base` (multilingual) with it caught 8 of 10 at 0.60 s per clip (10 of 10 with beam size 5, which the gate does not offer), `small.en` caught 10 of 10 at 1.9 s per clip; no variant fired on the 6 ordinary sentences; `WAKE_NAME_SIMILARITY` (0.75, 0.65, 0.55) made no difference. Settings only: the engine code is unchanged, and so is the greedy decoding (`beam_size=1`); a `STT_GATE_BEAM_SIZE` setting was not added. Limits of the evidence: one speaker, one room, 16 clips, no noise, TV or other voices, no ordinary sentence containing `oblivion`; the clips were cut by the maintainer's silence detection, not run through the live gate stream; not re-run live and not measured on a Raspberry Pi (a `base` gate is about twice the work of `tiny.en`). The prompt and the model are settings, so a deployment can put the old values back in `config/local/stt.toml`.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (live re-test of the new defaults; the offline result did not hold):** Live re-test of the new defaults, the same day (microphone, stt, tts, speaker and Brain from copies of the working trees, no ai-agent, no stepper; gate `base`, prompt `Oblivion 306.`, read from the new `.env.example` with no override): of 10 phrases said, 2 were dropped by Brain's echo guard (`utterance heard while the robot was speaking dropped`: the maintainer spoke while Brain was still saying its apology, which every accepted phrase triggers), and of the 8 that reached the gate **2 were accepted** (the gate wrote the phrase, exactly one `/process/batch` each) and 6 were not: in 4 the gate wrote only `What time is it?` or `What I miss it.` (name and code missing), in 1 `Or you'll be on 306. What time is it?` (name garbled, code present), and 1 more missed the name. The 3 ordinary sentences were ignored correctly (`The weather is nice today and I like coffee.`, `Please turn on the kitchen light.`, `Hello there, how are you doing today?`). That is 2 of 8 live against 8 of 10 offline on the same defaults, so the offline clips did not predict the live result. The utterances Brain received were 5.5 to 6.3 s long, which includes the 2 s of trailing silence that ends them. Not yet explained: the microphone's cutter (`utterance_segmenter.py`) has no pre-roll, so it starts an utterance at the chunk where the smoothed volume reaches the start threshold and can lose the opening of the first word (the name); the offline clips came from a continuous capture cut by a separate silence detector with 0.3 s of padding. A diagnostic that separates the two (cutter clipping the start, long trailing silence, or the speaker) has not been run. The defaults stay as set (they are no worse than before on the evidence of the table of this note), but the wake-phrase recognition is **not solved**.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (why the live result was lower than the offline one):** Diagnosis, same day (no services running; one continuous recording of the maintainer saying the phrase 10 times and 3 other sentences, cut offline with the microphone service's own `UtteranceSegmenter` and scored with the live gate settings, `base`, prompt `Oblivion 306.`, beam 1, and Brain's matcher): with the live default treatment the gate accepted **5 of 10** phrases (the live run gave 2 of 8). The cutter starts each utterance only **0.01 to 0.12 s after** the voiced onset (it has no pre-roll and measures a smoothed volume, `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING_FACTOR=0.1`), and the utterances are about 6 s long because the smoothed volume also decays slowly at the end. A grid on the same 10 phrases, moving only the start of the clip and the length of the tail, shows that the start is what matters (phrases accepted of 10; start relative to the voiced onset, tail in seconds): starting 0.1 to 0.2 s *inside* the phrase 5, 4, 3, 4 for tails 0.3, 1, 2, 3.5; exactly at the onset 9, 8, 8, 6; **0.1 s before the onset 9, 9, 9, 10**; 0.2 s before 9, 8, 8, 9; 0.5 s before 7, 8, 8, 6; 1.0 s before 9, 8, 8, 9. The tail matters little. Re-cuts with other treatments: smoothing off 8 of 10 (starts at the onset, utterances 4.5 s), smoothing factor 0.5 5 of 10, start factor 1 3 of 10, smoothing off + start factor 1 + no noise floor 8 of 10, a simulated pre-roll of 0.5 s 7 of 10 and of 1.0 s 6 of 10, the default cut with 1.5 s of tail trimmed 5 of 10; no variant fired on the 3 ordinary sentences (one of them in the recording). Not the cause: the gate model (the same `base` gets 9 or 10 of 10 when the clip starts a little before the phrase). So the live misses are mostly **the microphone's late start clipping the first sounds of `Oblivion`**, plus Brain's echo guard dropping a phrase said while the apology is spoken. Candidate fixes, both in the microphone service and neither applied: `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING=false` (settings only, about 8 of 10 and shorter utterances, at the cost of reacting to a single loud chunk) or a pre-roll of about 0.2 s (a code change there). Limits: one speaker, one room, 10 phrases (a cell of the grid moves by 2 or 3 between neighbours), not re-run live.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (the microphone fix):** a pre-roll was added to the microphone service (`MICROPHONE_PREROLL_SECONDS`, default 0.2 s: the latest quiet chunks are put in front of each utterance), the code change this note's diagnosis pointed to. It is tested with fake audio in the microphone repository (142 tests, 10 of 10 one-line mutations caught); its effect on the gate is checked offline on a new recording, and **not yet live through Brain**.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (verification of the pre-roll):** Offline check of the pre-roll on a second recording of the maintainer (10 phrases, 3 sentences, 110 s, no services running): each phrase was fed alone to a fresh copy of the real changed `UtteranceSegmenter` with the live default treatment, and what came out was scored with stt's gate (`base`, prompt `Oblivion 306.`, beam 1) and Brain's matcher. Phrases accepted of 10: pre-roll 0 (the old behaviour) 8, 0.1 s 10, 0.2 s (the new default) 9, 0.3 s 8, 0.5 s 9. That is neutral to slightly positive and **within the noise of 10 phrases**: this recording does not prove a gain, because the old cutter already did well on it (it started 0.0 to 0.1 s after the voiced onset, where the first recording's controlled sweep showed the cliff between starting 0.1 s inside the phrase, 3 to 5 of 10, and at or before the onset, 8 to 10 of 10). The pre-roll protects against the unlucky starts (a soft first sound, more smoothing delay) and was not seen to hurt. Also seen: with the default smoothed volume, phrases said about 5 s apart are **merged** into one utterance (the smoothed volume needs about 1.8 s to fall under the end threshold, plus the 2 s silence limit, so the end of an utterance is detected 3.8 s after the speech; one cut was 46 s long); with smoothing off every phrase was its own utterance (4.5 s each). Not yet run: the live round trip through Brain with the new microphone.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (third live test, both fixes):** Third live test, the same day, with both fixes (microphone pre-roll 0.2 s, gate `base` with prompt `Oblivion 306.`, read from the new `.env.example` files; microphone, stt, tts, speaker and Brain from copies of the working trees, no ai-agent, no stepper), phrases said 8 to 10 s apart: Brain received 11 utterances. **Every phrase that reached the gate was accepted: 4 of 4**, each followed by exactly one `/process/batch` (3.8 to 4 s with `small.en`, 31 to 33 characters back, then Brain's fixed apology because ai-agent was not running), and the one ordinary sentence that reached the gate (`The weather is nice today and I like coffee.`) was ignored correctly. The other **6 utterances never reached the gate**: Brain's echo guard dropped them (`utterance heard while the robot was speaking dropped`, 5.6 to 6.5 s each), because every accepted phrase makes the robot speak for a long time (its progress messages and the apology), and the maintainer said the next phrase or sentence during it (4 phrases and 2 ordinary sentences). So the live results with the same gate have been: first test 1 of 3, second 2 of 8, third 4 of 4 reaching the gate. The sample is small (4 phrases), the speaker and room are one, and the three runs differ in more than the two fixes (pace, what was said while the robot spoke), but this is the first run with no phrase lost to a misheard name. Not measured: a run with a real ai-agent (the robot's reply would be different and shorter or longer), a Raspberry Pi, other voices or noise.
