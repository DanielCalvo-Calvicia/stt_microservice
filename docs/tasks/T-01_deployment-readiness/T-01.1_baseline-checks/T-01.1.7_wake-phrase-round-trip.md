## Goal

Prove the flow the gate exists for, with the real microphone: the microphone cuts an utterance, Brain sends it to the gate (`/gate/...`), the gate hears the wake phrase, and Brain sends the **audio the gate returned** to the real engine's `/process/batch`. The gate has been tested only with fake engines.

## Read first

[../../../decisions/ADR-005-wake-phrase-gate.md](../../../decisions/ADR-005-wake-phrase-gate.md), [../../../api/api.md](../../../api/api.md) (gate routes), `deployment/docs/DEPLOYMENT.md` (the Brain settings `WAKE_PHRASE_ENABLED`, `WAKE_USE_GATE_STT`, `STT_GATE_PATH_PREFIX`).

## Files

None changed. The maintainer's `deployment/config/local/` files carry the settings (`STT_GATE_ENABLED = 1` on stt, `WAKE_PHRASE_ENABLED = 1` on Brain) and are restored afterwards.

## Steps

This needs a **real microphone** (and a speaker if tts and speaker are in the layout): the assistant prepares the commands and the maintainer runs them. Ask first. **Do not start the stepper**: leave it out of the run or ask before any movement.

1. Layout with microphone, Brain and stt (and tts, speaker, ai-agent only if the maintainer wants the answer spoken). Deploy with the tool (`deploy --host <machine>`); stt with the engine of the maintainer's choice (`local` avoids spending tokens).
2. Say a sentence **without** the wake phrase: the stt log shows the gate request and no request to `/process/batch`; Brain drops it.
3. Say a sentence **with** the phrase (`Oblivion 306`): the gate request is followed by one `/process/batch` request from Brain.
4. Read Brain's log (`oblivion.py logs --host <machine> -s brain --lines 100`) and stt's log for the two cases.

## Acceptance criteria

- The sentence without the phrase produces no batch request; the one with it produces exactly one.
- No utterance text or audio is in the stt log (lengths only).
- The result is written in *Notes* with what was said and what each log showed.

## Verification

The relevant log lines of Brain and stt, in *Notes*.

## Notes

Record the delay between the end of the utterance and Brain's decision with `tiny.en` on this machine; T-03.6 measures the same on a Raspberry Pi. If the gate misspells the phrase, `STT_GATE_PROMPT` and `base.en` are the knobs (no code change).

## Result (2026-11-09, run with the maintainer's yes and real microphone)

**Setup.** A scratch native deployment with the tool (`oblivion.py --config <scratch>`), made from copies of the working trees of microphone, stt, tts, speaker and Brain (7 min 53 s with the installs): `STT_ENGINE=local` (`small.en`), `STT_GATE_ENABLED=1`, Brain `WAKE_PHRASE_ENABLED=1`, `WAKE_USE_GATE_STT=1`, `WAKE_PHRASE=Oblivion 306`. **No ai-agent and no stepper** were started (ai-agent is only listed in the layout, which Brain's validation requires; nothing listened on 7998, so Brain's decision call failed and it spoke its fixed apology; no motor could move). The maintainer's real microphone and speaker were used; nothing left the machine and no token was spent. The services were stopped and the scratch folder removed afterwards.

**Round 1, gate `tiny.en`** (the default). (a) A 4.1 s clip: the gate returned no text (text length 0), nothing happened. (b) *"The weather is nice today and I like coffee."*: the gate heard `The weather is nice to read and I like coffee.` and Brain logged `utterance without the wake phrase ignored`; **no request to `/process/batch`**. (c) *"Oblivion 306, what time is it?"*: the gate heard `of Lulion 306. What time is it?`; the name is only 0.57 like "oblivion" (the threshold `WAKE_NAME_SIMILARITY` is 0.75), so Brain ignored it and sent **no** batch request. Delay from the end of the utterance to the gate's text: 0.84 s for a 6.2 s clip.

**Round 2, gate `base.en`** (`STT_GATE_MODEL=base.en`, stt and Brain redeployed in 18 s; the model downloaded by `prepare`). The phrase sentence was said three times. (1) The gate heard the phrase: Brain sent the gate's audio (200,704 bytes) to `POST /process/batch` (**exactly one** request, 3.96 s with `small.en`, 31 characters back), then tried ai-agent, failed with `All connection attempts failed`, and about 370 KB of the fixed apology went through TTS to the speaker. The gate took 1.2 s for a 6.3 s clip; from the end of the utterance to the real text about 5.2 s in all. (2) The gate heard `306, what time is it?` and (3) `3-0-6, what time is it?`: the name was **not** transcribed, so Brain ignored both and sent no batch request.

**Acceptance.** The sentence without the phrase produced no batch request (round 1b); the one with the phrase produced exactly one when the gate heard it (round 2.1), and none when it did not. No utterance text or audio is in the stt log: `Utterance transcribed` and `Batch transcription completed` carry lengths only (a search of both rounds for the spoken words found only the configured gate prompt `Oblivion`).

**What this shows and does not show.** The path works end to end with a real microphone: microphone, gate, Brain's decision, the real engine on the gate's own audio, TTS and speaker. The weak point is the gate's **recognition of the name**: `tiny.en` heard it 0 times out of 1 and `base.en` 1 time out of 3 (for this speaker, microphone and room; four attempts are too few for a rate). That is a tuning matter (`STT_GATE_MODEL`, `STT_GATE_PROMPT`, a different or longer wake phrase, `WAKE_NAME_SIMILARITY` only helps when the name is misspelled, not when it is missing), not a defect of the code. Not run: a longer series, a quieter or noisier room, a Raspberry Pi (postponed).

**Finding for Brain (not stt):** Brain's log line `utterance without the wake phrase ignored` carries `gate_heard="<the text>"`, so the transcript of speech not addressed to the robot is written to Brain's log. stt logs lengths only.

**Round 3, gate `base` with the prompt `Oblivion 306.` (the new defaults), same day:** (microphone, stt, tts, speaker and Brain from copies of the working trees, no ai-agent, no stepper; gate `base`, prompt `Oblivion 306.`, read from the new `.env.example` with no override): of 10 phrases said, 2 were dropped by Brain's echo guard (`utterance heard while the robot was speaking dropped`: the maintainer spoke while Brain was still saying its apology, which every accepted phrase triggers), and of the 8 that reached the gate **2 were accepted** (the gate wrote the phrase, exactly one `/process/batch` each) and 6 were not: in 4 the gate wrote only `What time is it?` or `What I miss it.` (name and code missing), in 1 `Or you'll be on 306. What time is it?` (name garbled, code present), and 1 more missed the name. The 3 ordinary sentences were ignored correctly (`The weather is nice today and I like coffee.`, `Please turn on the kitchen light.`, `Hello there, how are you doing today?`). That is 2 of 8 live against 8 of 10 offline on the same defaults, so the offline clips did not predict the live result. The utterances Brain received were 5.5 to 6.3 s long, which includes the 2 s of trailing silence that ends them. Not yet explained: the microphone's cutter (`utterance_segmenter.py`) has no pre-roll, so it starts an utterance at the chunk where the smoothed volume reaches the start threshold and can lose the opening of the first word (the name); the offline clips came from a continuous capture cut by a separate silence detector with 0.3 s of padding. A diagnostic that separates the two (cutter clipping the start, long trailing silence, or the speaker) has not been run.

**Diagnosis after round 3, same day:** (no services running; one continuous recording of the maintainer saying the phrase 10 times and 3 other sentences, cut offline with the microphone service's own `UtteranceSegmenter` and scored with the live gate settings, `base`, prompt `Oblivion 306.`, beam 1, and Brain's matcher): with the live default treatment the gate accepted **5 of 10** phrases (the live run gave 2 of 8). The cutter starts each utterance only **0.01 to 0.12 s after** the voiced onset (it has no pre-roll and measures a smoothed volume, `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING_FACTOR=0.1`), and the utterances are about 6 s long because the smoothed volume also decays slowly at the end. A grid on the same 10 phrases, moving only the start of the clip and the length of the tail, shows that the start is what matters (phrases accepted of 10; start relative to the voiced onset, tail in seconds): starting 0.1 to 0.2 s *inside* the phrase 5, 4, 3, 4 for tails 0.3, 1, 2, 3.5; exactly at the onset 9, 8, 8, 6; **0.1 s before the onset 9, 9, 9, 10**; 0.2 s before 9, 8, 8, 9; 0.5 s before 7, 8, 8, 6; 1.0 s before 9, 8, 8, 9. The tail matters little. Re-cuts with other treatments: smoothing off 8 of 10 (starts at the onset, utterances 4.5 s), smoothing factor 0.5 5 of 10, start factor 1 3 of 10, smoothing off + start factor 1 + no noise floor 8 of 10, a simulated pre-roll of 0.5 s 7 of 10 and of 1.0 s 6 of 10, the default cut with 1.5 s of tail trimmed 5 of 10; no variant fired on the 3 ordinary sentences (one of them in the recording). Not the cause: the gate model (the same `base` gets 9 or 10 of 10 when the clip starts a little before the phrase). So the live misses are mostly **the microphone's late start clipping the first sounds of `Oblivion`**, plus Brain's echo guard dropping a phrase said while the apology is spoken. Candidate fixes, both in the microphone service and neither applied: `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING=false` (settings only, about 8 of 10 and shorter utterances, at the cost of reacting to a single loud chunk) or a pre-roll of about 0.2 s (a code change there). Limits: one speaker, one room, 10 phrases (a cell of the grid moves by 2 or 3 between neighbours), not re-run live.

**Round 4, both fixes (microphone pre-roll and gate defaults), same day:** (microphone pre-roll 0.2 s, gate `base` with prompt `Oblivion 306.`, read from the new `.env.example` files; microphone, stt, tts, speaker and Brain from copies of the working trees, no ai-agent, no stepper), phrases said 8 to 10 s apart: Brain received 11 utterances. **Every phrase that reached the gate was accepted: 4 of 4**, each followed by exactly one `/process/batch` (3.8 to 4 s with `small.en`, 31 to 33 characters back, then Brain's fixed apology because ai-agent was not running), and the one ordinary sentence that reached the gate (`The weather is nice today and I like coffee.`) was ignored correctly. The other **6 utterances never reached the gate**: Brain's echo guard dropped them (`utterance heard while the robot was speaking dropped`, 5.6 to 6.5 s each), because every accepted phrase makes the robot speak for a long time (its progress messages and the apology), and the maintainer said the next phrase or sentence during it (4 phrases and 2 ordinary sentences). So the live results with the same gate have been: first test 1 of 3, second 2 of 8, third 4 of 4 reaching the gate. The sample is small (4 phrases), the speaker and room are one, and the three runs differ in more than the two fixes (pace, what was said while the robot spoke), but this is the first run with no phrase lost to a misheard name. Not measured: a run with a real ai-agent (the robot's reply would be different and shorter or longer), a Raspberry Pi, other voices or noise.
